What is Dalbitam?

Dalbitam (달빛암, "Moonlit Hermitage") is a 3D online temple modeled on the mountain temples of Korea. It is a quiet place to pause, pray or simply listen. You enter with a temple name, and you will see other visitors who are there right now. You can greet them, bow together and send a short blessing.

The sounds come from real Korean temples: the moktak, the evening service with bell, wooden fish and cloud gong, mountain streams and the sea.

Make a wish at a Korean temple

At temples across Korea, people come to quietly ask for something: good health for their parents, a passed exam, a fresh start. Dalbitam lets you do the same from anywhere, and every wish you leave stays in the night sky for other visitors to see.

Lotus wish lanterns · 연등

Koreans hang lotus lanterns at temples, most of all on Buddha's Birthday. Write your wish on one and it hangs over the courtyard. Older wishes float up into the sky above the temple.

108 bows · 108배

108 stands for the troubles of the human mind. With each bow you set one down. Finish 108 bows in the main hall and your own lantern lights up for everyone to see.

The moktak · 목탁

A hollow wooden "fish" that monks strike to keep the rhythm of chanting. A fish never closes its eyes, so it reminds you to stay awake and mindful.

The temple bell · 범종

Korean temples ring the great bronze bell at dawn and dusk to wake all beings. On New Year's Eve, Koreans ring it 33 times to welcome the new year.

Circling the pagoda · 탑돌이

Walk clockwise around the stone pagoda while holding your wish in mind, as visitors still do at temples all over Korea.

Roof tile wishes · 기와

When a temple re-tiles its roofs, visitors write wishes on the new tiles. Your wish becomes part of the temple itself.

Ring the temple bell

Strike the great bronze bell, modeled from a 3D scan of a real Korean temple bell.

Roof tile wishes

Write your wish on a roof tile (giwa), just as visitors do at temples in Korea.

Meditate and chant

Sit for ten breaths in a stone grotto, turn prayer beads or follow a chant with romanized text.

Places to explore

Beyond the main hall (Daeungjeon) and the stone pagoda, paths lead to the mountain spirit shrine, the Seven Stars hall, a stone grotto inspired by Seokguram, a sutra library inspired by Janggyeong Panjeon, a lotus pond inspired by Wolji, Moonlight Peak and a lantern path down to the sea.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a wish at a Korean temple?
At Korean temples, people write a wish on a lotus lantern or a roof tile, bow, and ring the temple bell. At Dalbitam you write your wish on a lotus lantern online, then do 108 bows in the main hall to light it up.
What is an online temple?
An online temple is a place you can visit through your screen to pray, meditate or simply rest. Dalbitam is a 3D Korean Buddhist mountain temple you walk through in your web browser, together with whoever else is visiting at the same time.
Is Dalbitam free?
Yes. Dalbitam is free to visit. There is nothing to download and no account to create.
Do I need to be Buddhist?
No. Anyone is welcome. Many visitors come to take a quiet break, listen to temple sounds or learn about Korean temple culture.
Does it work on my phone?
Yes. Dalbitam runs in mobile and desktop browsers. You can also add it to your home screen like an app.
